Fountains pens are nice if you write by hand a lot. When I was taking humanities classes, I would get assigned 30 page of readings a week -- which meant a ton of summarizing and note taking. I started out using a ballpoint pen, but I would get tired after a few pages of writing. I decided to invest in a fountain pen, which allowed me to write with a lighter grip. It was a quality of life improvement. It made writing feel pleasurable (similar to how I feel when I see the PDF produced by a LaTeX file). I started with a $15 Japanese Pilot Metropolitan (not a good pen IMO, the fine nib was too scratchy for me) but eventually moved to a $30 Taiwanese TWSBI ECO and nowadays my go-to is a $30 German Lamy Safari. ($200 pens are a luxury; the sweet spot is ~$30; also Western pens and Asian pens are different in their nib sizing). Those of you who are using BiC pens -- you can do so much better. However, I will admit that gel pens come pretty close, so if you don't care for fountain pens, gel pens should be your go to.

Pilot Metropolitan: Likes itTWSBI Eco: Likes it
In my view, Lamy Safari is way overrated. Don't get me wrong, it's far from bad, a pretty consistent pen for the price. But the ink dries fast-ish if you don't use it, and especially, the cartridges are proprietary so you're tied to the brand's cartridges and ink colors (there's a piston converter, but converters, meh). A Pilot Metropolitan is approximately the same price and at least in my view, it's better as it uses standard international cartridges. A TWSBI Eco is more expensive, but not by that much, and it's an absolute reliability monster.
