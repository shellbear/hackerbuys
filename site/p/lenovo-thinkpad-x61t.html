> It reads like this article comes from a parallel universe I think you do not understand what Raspbian/PiOS is and what it is for. The Pi started out in 2012 as a real Linux computer for kids to play with Python, at a price of £25. (It's British.) It had 1 slow ARM core and 512MB of RAM. Even in 2012 it was hard to run a graphical Linux desktop in 512MB RAM. Debian ran very poorly, for 2 reasons. 1. It's big and bloated. Still is. 2. The Debian developers assumed any Arm v6 kit would not have a hardware FPU. So, Debian for Arm v6 only did software floating point. Even slower. The Pi had an FPU. So Mike Thompson and Pete Green recompiled all of Debian. They make 2 global changes: * Arm v6 binaries with FPU support, an arch called `hardfp` * An extremely cut-down Debian desktop with an extremely cut-down LXDE desktop to run with room to spare in half a gig with only slow SD card available for swap. And they wrapped that in a dead easy installer for kids which asks no difficult questions. It doesn't ask about netboot or which desktop or what optional components you want. It asks your name, and not much else. And it gives a usable OS image that was a gig or so in size and used under 200MB of RAM when running X11. That is what Raspbian was: a super tiny super simple build of Debian for this specific kit: Arm v6 binaries with hardware FPU compiled in, plus a fancy GPU, and very little RAM and slow storage.
