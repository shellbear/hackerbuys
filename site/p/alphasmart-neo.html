The author should check out the AlphaSmart line of devices made into the early 2000s, especially the early pre-PalmOS devices! I was kind of surprised not to see any on the list. My Neo2 is basically a very simple buffer you can type into and dump into a computer later. It transmits to computer by emulating a USB keyboard and dumping the contents keystroke by keystroke into whatever program you happen to have running. In addition though it has a very simple typing tutor. There's not much to it, it's no true Mavis Beacon alternative but it's a fun little addition to such a simple device. I actually work with a number of former AlphaSmart employees, which is funny because I bought my Neo2 as a writing aid before I knew any of this. About 10 years ago I started seeing writing as a creative practice very similar to playing an instrument. If you want to get good at playing an instrument there's no shortcut to replace playing a LOT. I found that when I wrote on a computer I was too distracted by where the writing would go. I was conscious of writing for an audience, and would get too caught up on saying things in a particular way- stopping starting deleting rewriting. I ended up getting a Selectric III, the pinnacle of mechanical typewriter achievement. It is a beautiful, heavy, loud machine. While there is a corrective function, I've never used it. Writing on a typewriter is like a one way street- words come out of your hands and can't be put back in. I was focused on pushing out as many pages as I could, practicing on the instrument for an hour a day or more. It changed my writing style and helped me discover a personal voice. I still use the Selectric for writing letters to friends, and for "daily driver" writing use an Alphasmart NEO (another beautiful purpose-built machine). Edit to comment on TFA: I use an "IBM Selectric Script" ball. A sort of light cursive.
