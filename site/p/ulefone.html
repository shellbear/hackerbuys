Likes itOwned 3 yearsStill going
I bought a cheap(£150 at the time) phone 3-4 years ago called "Ulefone" where in a sense the 'case' is built in to the phone. It is marketed as a phone for handymen or outdoor functions. It is a bit bigger with protective shell around it is bulky, but withstood all the drops that a typical phone would break. There is some flaring around the screen and camera that it prevents most of scratches. The back has some sort of hard rubber but it held up well. I've only had to replace the screen protector within 3 years as the scuffs and marks made it difficult to see in well-lit environment.
embed_tinkerer, 2025-09-22 in “Vapor chamber tech keeps iPhone 17 Pro cool”
Doesn’t like itOwned 1 year
I agree with their views on modularity and would like to see this legislated for all devices. I recently bought a little boom-box and one notable problem with it is the lack of a user serviceable battery. Once that battery fails most people will just throw it in the bin. "Old School" boom boxes had a removable panel to install batteries. I have two Android phones I have been tinkering around with and noticed that the one with the 10,000mAh battery uleFone has been discharging a little bit faster each week, so it won't be long before it goes in the bin and I've only had it about a year. To Fairphones point disposable smart phones can't be very good on the environment. As a side note, I would love to see Fairphone also include a de-googled image by default on their devices that supports open source application repositories. Maybe F-Droid? I do not know how many people depend on the Google services or could be weened off of it or automatically migrated to alternative services.
LinuxBender, 2023-09-13 in “Apple thinks modularity hinders device longevity. We don’t”
Neutral
Stock Android phone free of bloatware? I've not seen any. There are growing collections of debloat scripts that use adb and do not require rooting the phone but it's hit and miss per model as to completeness and not breaking the OS startup. Some vendors lock down their bloat so that rooting is required and in my opinion those should get returned / refunded and the product reviews on sites such as Amazon should warn others. I've returned a few. My uleFone had the least cruft on it and the least lockdown but still uses upwards of 4GB ram after my first pass so I still have a long way to go.
LinuxBender, 2023-06-30 in “Ask HN: Stock Android phone free of bloatware?”
