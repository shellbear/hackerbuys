I've got a Blueair one that I was given, which thankfully has cheap filters available for on Amazon. These are otherwise REALLY expensive. The noise is nice, and it helps with dust in the bedroom. But I recently got a Yogasleep Dohm Classic [1] and it really does sound better. Placed across the bedroom, with the two controls adjusted just... right... it makes that really nice water-rushing proper white noise sound. I do not like electronic ones at all, but there's something about this one which uses an actual fan + ports for the noise that is great. I've also put it on a smart switch that I control from Home Assistant and this has made it even better. Specifically, I have an automation which gradually fades in some lights beginning 15 minutes before my alarm actually goes off [2]. I added turning off the white noise machine to the start of lights fading, and between the gradual light and sound change, I just sort-of wake up rather naturally a few minutes before the alarm noise starts. (Just the lights were fine before, but the cessation of white noise adds a little more. And a "Bed Time" automation shuts off all the lights in the house, turns on the white noise machine, etc.) [1] https://yogasleep.com/products/dohmclassic [2] https://nuxx.net/blog/2026/01/28/hue-a-better-sunrise-bulb/

I bought a pair of sensors based on the HN mentions, and so far I'm pretty happy. I was mainly interested in their PM sensor, which isn't on your list of complaints, and works quite well. I discovered my rangehood, which doesn't have an outdoor exhaust, is even more useless than I had imagined. I bought a HEPA filter for my kitchen to run while I'm cooking (a Blueair based on other HN comments) and based on the PM sensor readings of the AirGradient the HEPA filter is quite effective. I did have to configure the CO₂ sensor recalibration period to keep the numbers sensible. It wasn't so easy to set different recalibration periods for the indoor and outdoor sensors, but with some curl commands, I was able to get things configured to my liking. I do appreciate that, in principle, I can edit the firmware to my liking in the future.

I've done so much research about air purifiers that I think I could do a thesis if I were in academia. The vast majority of these devices fall under one category: rubbish. Lots of gimmicks performed when it comes to efficacy. Bending reality with borderline claims or inventing useless terms that mean nothing. If you are serious about indoor air quality, start with IQAir. Their products are bulky, contain multiple filters and you know that you'll be able to get replacement filters 5 years later. Blueair has some reasonable products too (ignore the smaller, cheap product lines).
