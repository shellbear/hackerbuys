Doesn’t like it
It’s definitely a weird scenario. It seems everyone in the Durable Medical Equipment space for diabetes stuff just wants everything to be a smartphone app now- which is fine honestly, it’s convenient for those that want to use it, but I’ve been informed that many manufacturers (at least a good number of Continuous Glucose Monitor makers) have completely forgone native controllers these days. Omnipod just seems to have said “we’ll make one program because maintaining two could be problematic for consistency (and probably certification) reasons”, and then made the logical-enough extrapolation that their native controller can just be an android device. They just chose a cheap/shit one- which is fair! It runs AN app! But it also means I have to keep USB A to C chargers on me 24/7 :/ I’m just glad they at least do that, as smartphone-only options are problematic for a number of reasons, among them being the lack of ability to bring them into restricted areas. I hadn’t thought about it before, but it’d also be a problem for events where they use those stupid yondr bags, or even schools that use them now. I guess you could just get your own little junkpile android for the sole purpose of being a controller, but still. sigh

If you like this, you might like the Omnipod teardown as well. It's what pairs with continuous glucose monitors. It's an entire insulin pump that you throw away every three days, so the engineering to make the parts that cheap is pretty amazing. They don't waste money on any motors. they just ratchet a worm gear with something called muscle wire. It's a nicanol alloy that shrinks when current heats it. You hear a click click click as it injects a 20th of a unit of insulin at a time. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e2MQUUkubgs
