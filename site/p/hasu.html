I've seen this but haven't bit. I've been many laps through the keyboard obsession. I learned Dvorak on a Matias Half Keyboard, skeptical of their brain mirror theory, then tried it out of curiousity on a full keyboard one day. My other hand just knew. A few years later, I switched back to QWERTY. Optimizing the alphabet isn't the bottleneck. I owned a FingerWorks TouchStream keyboard, before the company was sold to Apple to become the foundation of their gesture interfaces. I never got the hang of no tactile feedback, and sold it at cost, a mistake. They're now valuable collector's items. I used to use three Kinesis Advantage sculpted ergonomic keyboards. Their internal electronics was dated and unstable; I gave up. I recently went way down the mechanical/ergonomic rabbit hole, trying designs such as the Ergodox EZ, Planck EZ, GergoPlex, and various more standard 65% designs with a variety of switch choices. Then I tried a Topre rubber dome keyboard. They're called "endgame" for a reason: They're obsession killers. They're indisputably a better feel than any mechanical switch, and there's nothing one can to do customize the hardware. No more forum pictures of your pretty keycaps because you can't follow the seminar talk on lubing stems. Just learn to type better. So I bought four Leopold FC660C Topre Silent 45g keyboards, and Hasu replacement controllers supporting QMK and related custom firmware. Colorful keycaps is high school obsession.
