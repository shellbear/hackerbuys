Existenceblinks, 2022-12-02 in “Smartphones wiped out 97% of the compact camera market”
Neutral
Physics are a limitation but not a blocker. The only major limitation imho is focal length. There's only so much optical zoom you can fit into the smartphone form factor. AI post processing will probably make digital "zoom" much less of an issue in the near future. I'm an amateur photographer. I do mostly street and architecture with a bit of landscape. Right now my primary cameras are an ASP-C DSLR, a Sony RX100, as well as a selection of 35mm, MF, and instant film cameras. All of these bring different strengths to the table but my DSLR and RX100 stay at home more often than not these days. The in-camera processing just can't compare to smartphones. The fact that it is possible to apply the similar processing to DSLR or full-frame mirrorless images doesn't make those cameras any less expensive, heavy, or bulky. For most people, those are the points that matter. The destination of most images is social media more often than 8x10s, the ever shrinking quality advantages offered by "proper" cameras are outweighed by the convenience of having something more than adequate in their pocket at all times. After all, as the cliched advice goes: "The best camera is the one you have with you.", Or for old film photojournalists: "F8 and be there."

felizuno, 2022-05-18 in “Using a "proper" camera as a webcam”
Likes it
The key to this is really to find the "good enough" solution. For me that was a Sony RX100 Mk V that I already owned plugged into a £25 USB-HDMI capture card. No software required. There is an option to remove the default overlay just for mini-HDMI output in the camera settings. The increase in quality compared to even the "high-end" webcams is significant, with only a minor increase in complexity. I think if your solution requires you to start faffing around with proprietary camera software or OBS then you've gone too far. I also agree with other commenters in that a higher quality microphone is far more important. I personally use a Rode Procaster into a Focusrite Scarlett Solo USB interface, but I also use the microphone for professional use. Even something as simple as an external USB microphone (e.g. AT2020USB+ [1]) is going be a massive improvement to *the people who need to listen to you*. [1] https://www.audio-technica.com/en-gb/at2020usb

I have used an Olympus E-P2 and Sony RX100. These are good cameras, but not great, and not approaching a Leica M for one reason: the viewfinders (or lack thereof). An optical viewfinder is an essential feature which connects the shooter to the subject in a way that cannot be matched by an LCD. And while the beloved Fuji X100(s) has an optical viewfinder, it has a pathetic 90% coverage and 0.5x magnification. This makes a big, big difference in the active, engaged, photo-journalistic work that Leica is known for. For six years I shot with an Olympus E-1 DSLR. It's lame by today's digital standards, with only 5MP, but it was far better as a tool for making images than any of these no-, small-, or electronic viewfinder cameras.
