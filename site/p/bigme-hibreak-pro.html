I'm a huge fan of my e-ink phone for similar reasons, it's a full-fat (if fairly anaemic) Android phone with an e-ink screen as opposed to a 'minimal phone'. It's another way of achieving something similar, as well as making everything greyscale by nature it wrecks the contrast and while it can do video it really shouldn't! Most distracting apps are usable but a miserable UX, but long-form text is a genuine joy to read on it in a way that'd be absolutely miserable on a normal phone; the e-ink and the fact it works without the frontlight on makes a huge difference. Firefox tends to fare better than native apps in my experience, so when I write software for it I target the browser. Like the article suggests I'm putting structural incentives and disincentives towards the behaviours I want to control rather than relying on willpower, which is a scarce resource for me! I have a Bigme Hibreak Pro for anyone who's interested, I got the monochrome version but there's a colour one too. I've made life harder for myself be de-googling it, but I've no doubt for most people it's a perfectly serviceable primary device too especially if you don't do this.
