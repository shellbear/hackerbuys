Cables/bad connections are the number one source of errors reported in my experience, and encountering these is to be expected when hooking enough things up for a project. These are _usually_ found in the "Read" or "Write" columns, meaning the hardware involved caught the errors when the data over whatever serial protocol failed it's checksum (CRC32 for SATA I believe), the transmission was then resent and the error reported to the OS/ZFS. These errors only show up for a specific disk. Reseating the connections, making sure there are no harsh bends, and finally replacing the cable is the fix. Rarely, reported checksum errors are stopped because of replacing a cable. I don't have the depth of technical knowledge to understand why this happens. Then you have Controllers/HBA's/Backplanes either going bad, or more often overheating. Put a fan on your HBA. These errors can show up across multiple disks Bad/weak/noisy power supplies, and system overclocking that effects the PCIe bus and attached (like messing with BCLK) can cause havoc as well. Sometimes a CPU/system might have an undocumented bug specific to that system that can cause sanity loss trying to figure out WTF is going on. I've also noticed (and have come across a few others as well) that if you have enough Samsung SATA 860 EVOs, a particular one may end up persistently generating just a few errors every so often. I don't recall if these were read/write errors or a checksum error, but a clear and scrub will resolve thes…
