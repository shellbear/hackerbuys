Likes it
I haven't seen much mention of APFS yet. APFS is mandatory now. I recently used OCLP to coerce macOS 15 "Sequoia" onto my now-elderly 2015 27" Retina iMac. It's a capable machine: 4 cores, 32GB RAM, 1TB SSD as the system drive and 8TB HDD for data. I kept my home directory on the HFS+ HDD. So, fast OS, slower but very capacious personal drive. It was a very simple hack and everything automatically obeyed. Worked fine up to Monterey (macOS 13), the last supported version. Not now. Lots of apps wouldn't run and the OS couldn't save lots of settings. I had to go through the first-login wizard every single time. In the end I moved my home directory to the system volume on the SSD, and then moved all the big folders back out to HFS+ HDD (Documents, Downloads, Pictures, Music, Videos, etc.) This took 2 days of work but it did the job. Everything works now and the machine is noticeably faster, but now, I'm using 2x more SSD space and less HDD space. The point being that the POSIX texts require setting classic Unix values on HFS+ drives, and I am not sure all of those still work on APFS, and that will make passing harder. I think they might do it anyway, and soon, macOS 27, on Arm64 only, might pass... but in an even more wildly nonstandard config.
