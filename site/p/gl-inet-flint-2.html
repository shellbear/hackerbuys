I actually use the non-mobile Flint 2 myself at home, and it's one of the devices in my tailnet. I worked with their engineers on the forums to get better IPv6 support for their WireGuard tunnels. Running both Tailscale and WireGuard on it can mess up the routing at times though, so I prefer to stick to just either or. It's a bit unfortunate they decided to go with Broadcom for their Flint 3 router, since Broadcom is known to not play well with open source. One of the reasons I got Flint 2 was its Mediatek chip, since stock OpenWRT support for that should get reasonably good eventually. They're all still way more open than TP-Link Decos.
juhovh, 2025-09-10 in “Show HN: TailGuard – Bridge your WireGuard router into Tailscale via a container”

I have had good results with the GL.iNet routers too, but the "supported by openwrt" just means that GL.iNet pulled an old version of OpenWRT and hacked it up until it worked on their board instead of making their own OEM firmware. They're not upstreaming their patches or collaborating with OpenWRT, just kind of leeching off them. I'm not even sure you can install vanilla OpenWRT on the Flint 2 yet. In contrast, this is made by and with the OpenWRT dev team!

Hey, I think this is important, Flint 3 relies on a Qualcomm chipset, so, at least some weeks ago, no vanilla OpenWRT was available for it (Qualcomm kernel). On the other hand, if you can live with Wifi 6 and only 2x2.5Gbps ports (and 4x1Gbps), Flint 2 is powered with a Mediatek chipset, that runs a 100% vanilla OpenWRT. Both are 1GB RAM, 8GB emmc little beasts that can even run some docker containers. IMHO Flint2 is in the top 5 for SOHO OpenWRT supported routers
