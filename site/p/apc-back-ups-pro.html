No one on this site has a large enough sample size to speak authoritatively about the quality of consumer-oriented UPSes. In my home right now, I have an APC (Schneider) Back-UPS Pro 1000, Tripp Lite (Eaton) AVR550U, and CyberPower CP1500PFCLCDa. I purchased the APC in April 2017, the CyberPower in Nov 2020, and the Tripp Lite in Sep 2021. The APC was originally for all my IT gear but for some reason that load caused it to cycle to battery at random times. I replaced it with the CyberPower which works fine with my IT gear. Meanwhile the APC works fine with my AV gear where I re-purposed it. Both of those are still on their original batteries which I test every 6 months. The Tripp Lite I use at my desk and I also test it every 6 months, but last month the power went out and the Tripp Lite immediately shut down. The battery had failed w/o warning and the Tripp Lite battery-monitoring is apparently useless since it still thought the battery was good when power was restored, but an actual load test proved otherwise. When I replaced the battery, the Tripp Lite had a cheap Chinese battery in it. I replaced it with a Duracell (manufactured in Vietnam). I've had less trouble with non-Chinese UPS batteries. Another fun thing: Tripp Lite has reused the model "AVR550U" for three different UPSes all which use a different battery. Turns out my version uses the quite common APC RBC2 sized 7Ah battery. Which is all to say: I've had a lot of UPSes over the years and you can't make any claims…
js2, 2024-02-23 in “I don't think the cheapest APC Back-UPS units can be monitored except in Windows”

I had one of these with an extra battery https://www.bestbuy.com/site/apc-back-ups-pro-1000va-battery... back in the day I had slow ADSL (2 Mbit/sec) from the phone company central office so my DSL would stay up when the power went out. That system could keep my DSL modem and cordless phone up and running for about two days or so, so with laptops and tablets I'd have 21st century comms for a weekend when the power goes out. They upgraded my DSL to ADSL2 served from a fiber node a mile or so from my house, now I get highly reliable and consistent 20 Mbit/sec, except when the power goes out in which case the node goes down. I donated the UPS to my local reuse center because there wasn't much point in it. I have a small tower computer I use as a server and thought about plugging it into the UPS but decided it wasn't worth the trouble because the runtime wouldn't be good. It would probably be better than 5 minutes, but it's not "run through a power outage", it is, "have a short period of terror in which to save your documents". Keeping the DSL up was "life-changing" though because it meant I could still keep in touch with the people at work.
